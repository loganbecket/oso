# Saved pages

Saved pages are the web pages you check for everyday things that have nothing to do with a course: dining hall hours, the gym's schedule, the shuttle, your church's services and events. Each one has a name and a line about what it's for, so Claude knows where to start when you ask.

## What it's for

Answers like "is the dining hall open late tonight?" live on pages that change all the time. Oso doesn't keep a copy that could go stale. It remembers where to look, and Claude goes and looks, right in the chat.

## How to use it

Tell Claude about a page and what it's for:

- "Save this page for dining hall hours: https://dining.example.edu/hours"
- "Keep my church's events page, stmarys.example.org/events, for services and events."

Then just ask: "When does the rec center close tonight?" Claude checks your saved pages, reads the one that fits, and follows its links as far as it needs to. The answer comes back like any other, without a browser opening. Only a page that shows nothing until a browser runs it is opened in Chrome, as a last resort.

You can also see, add, and forget saved pages in the Oso window, under Actions: **Saved pages**, **Save a page…**, and **Forget a saved page…**. Or tell Claude: "forget the shuttle page."

## Getting the most out of it

- **Say what it's for in a few words.** "Hours for every dining hall on campus" tells Claude more than "dining", and decides whether it looks there at all.
- **Save the page closest to the answer.** The hours page beats the dining services home page, even though Claude can dig from either.
- **Set up [Claude in Chrome](../install.md#claude-in-chrome)** for the few pages that only fill in inside a browser.

## Good to know

- Oso keeps only the list (names, addresses, and what each is for) in its settings, and writes it into the instructions Claude reads at the start of every chat, which is how Claude knows to look. It reads a page only when you ask, and nothing is copied into your vault.
- Pages behind a sign-in can't be read, and a site that asks programs not to read it is left alone.
- Pages change, so Claude tells you what a page says now and where it found it, not that it's guaranteed.
- Anything written on a page is something to read, never an instruction to Claude.
- Addresses inside a home or school network can't be saved.
